## 2. Attack on what is new

### A. Write-then-verdict does not guarantee exactly-one-wire

It can support **at-most-one original-or-replacement emission**, subject to explicit failure assumptions. It cannot guarantee exactly one wire delivery under every crash outcome:

- Crash before successful TUN write: zero deliveries.
- Successful write: kernel acceptance is not proof of eventual wire transmission.
- Crash after write: avoiding a duplicate requires the original never subsequently receiving ACCEPT, including during recovery.
- A reader/worker hang with its socket still open is not equivalent to socket destruction.

**NFQUEUE does not supply the generic per-packet timeout asserted by this plan.** A packet may remain queued until verdict or queue teardown. Specify a supervisor deadline and actual teardown mechanism; “timeout/release” is not an implementation.

The more serious defect is authorization:

> REINJECT writes first, but generation linearizes at verdict issue.

Once TUN accepts the replacement, dropping the original cannot retract that replacement. Demotion between write and DROP therefore permits an emission before the plan’s claimed authorization check.

**Required:** separate disposition commit points. ACCEPT commits at its defined verdict submission boundary; REINJECT commits at TUN submission. Both require the appropriate generation fence. A DROP verdict after injection is cleanup, not authorization.

Also, “verdict issue” must distinguish attempted, successful, and uncertain submission.

### B. REINJECT does reintroduce a re-entry design risk

Not automatically the old half-admission bug: if the exclusion machinery actually covers every TUN identity and alias, refusing ingress admission is coherent.

But exclusion does not prove correct forwarding:

- A **per-worker TUN** cannot simultaneously have one fixed binding to several independent routing instances without an additional steering mechanism.
- T3 therefore needs a worker×instance TUN model or an explicit alternative, including lifecycle and resource bounds.
- A packet captured at FORWARD has already traversed earlier kernel processing. Reinjection starts another ingress traversal. The plan must account for routing, TTL/hop-limit handling, conntrack/NAT, firewall hooks, marks, and ICMP behavior across both traversals.
- Comparing the kernel FIB with the adjudicated route does not guarantee that either ACCEPT or REINJECT realizes the adjudicated egress under policy routing, ECMP, or concurrent route changes.

A binding-plan exclusion is necessary. It is not a re-entry forwarding contract.

### C. Shadow ACCEPT-always plus stamped phase is implementable—but not as an unconditional guarantee

An immutable phase token is straightforward for packets already received by userspace. The unresolved questions are **where phase is sampled and what the flip means**.

A packet waiting in the kernel queue has no userspace phase stamp. Sampling on receive is implementable, but differs from sampling at diversion.

Allowing old shadow work to complete after the flip means:

1. Enforcement activation is not immediately a strict enforcement boundary.
2. Invalidating shadow sessions once at the flip is insufficient: old work can create sessions afterward.
3. Generation cancellation says DROP; shadow says unconditional ACCEPT. Precedence is unspecified.

Choose drain-before-activation or cancel-before-activation, or explicitly expose a transitional state with epoch-restricted session publication. Shadow sessions should also not silently become active forwarding/NAT state.

T14’s “never forward unadjudicated” cannot be treated as equivalent to enforcement in ACCEPT-always shadow. Define the intended observation and failure guarantees separately.

### D. Queue-instance generation binding is possible; the current text moves part of the stamping problem

A genuinely distinct live queue per epoch identifies packets already enqueued to that queue. That is a meaningful improvement.

But “new queue” needs concrete semantics:

- Queue-number allocation and reuse quarantine.
- Atomic rule replacement ordering.
- Old listener lifetime and queue destruction.
- Outstanding userspace commands and verdict identifiers.
- Classification of packets that entered the network stack before rotation but reach the hook afterward.
- Coordination with policy/RG snapshot publication.

A queue identifies the epoch selected at the diversion boundary—not inherently the packet’s earlier ingress epoch.

Old userspace verdict work must never address a replacement queue through a recycled identifier. Queue-instance binding is a valid design technique, not a substitute for this lifecycle protocol.

### E. T22 thresholds are executable numbers, not justified acceptance criteria

There is no derivation for **80%** or **250 µs**. Re-measuring the baseline establishes a denominator; it does not ratify an acceptable regression.

Missing definitions include:

- Packet-size distribution, tunnel count, flows, bidirectional load.
- ACCEPT/REINJECT mix, NAT, fragments, and host-bound traffic.
- Offered load and permitted loss.
- Whether throughput is useful delivered traffic rather than processed/dropped traffic.
- Latency measurement method, load point, and handling of dropped packets.
- Repeated-run variability and non-tunnel interference limits.

The 100 µs flush cap does not bound worker backlog, ordered verdict waiting, scheduler delay, or reassembly. The 250 µs claim needs those qualifications.

**New factual error:** mainline NFQUEUE has batch-verdict support. Its cumulative verdict semantics are not arbitrary mixed per-packet disposition, but “no mainline batched-verdict NFQUEUE API” is false. Price the chosen API shape; do not justify it by denying an existing capability.

## 3. Additional new holes

### Blocking: capture-order verdicts conflict with fragment completion

The plan requires all verdicts to issue in capture order, while fragments remain held until completion.

An incomplete early datagram therefore blocks later unrelated completed traffic. With bounded in-flight capacity, later fragments needed for completion may themselves be refused. This creates head-of-line blocking and potentially prevents progress until expiry.

Specify an out-of-order verdict policy and bounded fragment ownership separate from ordinary batch slots. Per-tunnel queues do not solve this within a tunnel.

### Blocking: whole-batch dispatch contradicts flow affinity

An ordinary capture batch contains multiple flows with potentially different worker owners. “Batch wholly on one worker” and “reuse physical-path dispatch on the flow key” cannot both apply without constructing owner-homogeneous batches.

Define partitioning, per-owner flush timing, reverse-flow resolution, and fragment-to-session ownership transfer.

### Blocking: fragment semantics still permit inspection/forwarding disagreement

“First-seen bytes win” plus dropping later overlapping fragments can leave holes in the forwarded fragment set. Forwarding overlaps instead risks downstream reconstruction differing from worker inspection.

IPv6 overlap handling cannot be deferred as an implementation detail while claiming an approved cross-family contract. Specify whole-datagram rejection or another coherent, protocol-correct disposition.

Also validate hook placement against actual kernel defragmentation: an INPUT/FORWARD queue cannot simply assume it sees original fragments when earlier defragmentation is active.

### Major: resource promises remain mutually unpriced

At 1,024 datagrams × 64 KB, reassembly alone permits roughly **64 MiB per tunnel**, before retained original fragments, queue objects, and slabs. Aggregate memory and thread bounds are absent; the “stated tunnel max” is never stated.

The 2-second fragment hold also needs reconciliation with queue depth, overload recovery, and T22 latency accounting.

## Final assessment

**NEEDS-MAJOR.** Retain NFQUEUE as a research candidate, but do not approve implementation against these invariants yet.

Required revisions are architectural: disposition-specific commit points, an executable queue/phase lifecycle, a complete TUN forwarding contract, and compatible fragment/worker scheduling. More named tests cannot repair contradictory expected behavior.
