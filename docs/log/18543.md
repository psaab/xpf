# Issue #18543 — an interrupted iperf3 json-stream client was scored completed at 0.0 Gbps

## Origin: the "rejoin collapse" investigation (2026-10-30)

The report: in `make test-failover`, after the crashed fw0 rejoins as
secondary, throughput through fw1 collapses for about 60 s until the RG1
failback, and some streams starve. Four runs were read: integ7a loss/tcp,
integ7b tcp and diag7b loss.

**Finding 1: the plateau is the harness's own split-RG window, not a
post-rejoin defect.** In all four runs the collapse starts when
`ha_run_rg1_failback_slice` (#11581) makes its RG1-only failover request. It
ends at the RG0/RG2 request. That gives a 63–73 s window with RG1 (WAN) on
node0 and RG2 (LAN) on node1:

| run | t0 (UTC) | RG1 request → collapse | RG0/RG2 request → recovery | plateau |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| integ7a loss `1481034` | 06:08:59 | fw1 RG1 demote 06:11:09.03, fw0 activate 06:11:09.69 → t=132 | fw0 RG0 06:12:18.57, RG2 06:12:19.15 → t=201 | 1.4–1.7 Gbit/s |
| integ7a tcp `1479181` | 06:08:36 | phase-pre 06:11:33 → t=179 | post-sampler 06:12:25 → t=243 | 7.2 Gbit/s |
| integ7b tcp `1902225` | 06:53:29 | fw1 RG1 demote 06:55:26.40 → t=119 | fw1 RG0/RG2 demote 06:56:32.36/.73 → t=186 | 12–13 Gbit/s |
| diag7b loss `2139278` | 07:10:22 | phase-pre 07:12:34 → t=135 | post-sampler 07:13:37 → t=209 | 3.1–3.6 Gbit/s |

The harness capture counters (`test-failover-ha-<pid>/*.interfaces`) show
every LAN packet on the LAN owner leaving on the copy-mode virtio fabric slot
(`shared-disabled="... uses virtio_net"`). On integ7a loss that was about
160 kpps on fw1's fabric TX and fw0's fabric RX, with WAN TX on fw1 at 0. This
is the documented split-RG fabric ceiling (`docs/fabric-performance-optimizations.md`:
2.0–3.5 Gbps on loss). RTT inflated to 60–200 ms because the streams were
cwnd-clamped at about 4.2 MB behind a deep lossless queue.

Control on tcp, integ7b build, under the tcp lock, with **no crash and no
rejoin**: steady 8-stream load, then RG1 alone moved to node1, then back.
The rate went 38.6–38.9 → 5.9–6.0 → 37–39 Gbit/s, exactly bounded by the two
requests (07:27:21.2 and 07:27:47.8). fw0 sent 500 kpps of LAN traffic out its
copy-mode fabric; fw1 received 521 kpps on the fabric and sent it out its WAN.
After the return, fw0 forwarded 3.39 Mpps directly. No stream died in the
control.

**Finding 2: the starved streams are dead streams.** They show 0 bytes,
`snd_cwnd` of 1 MSS and RTO retransmits at about +1, 3, 7, 15, 30, 60 and
115 s, none answered. They stay dead even after every RG is back on fw0.
This is the split-RG failback class owned by 18531/18535. integ7a lost 3–4
streams at the RG1 move. integ7b, which carries 6439c2baef, lost none at the
RG1 move but 5 at the RG0/RG2 move. diag7b lost 3 at the RG0/RG2 move. The
pure split control lost none, so the deaths need the crash/rejoin import
state.

**Finding 3 (this issue): the tcp gates read the dead streams as "0.0 Gbps".**
With blackholed streams the client hangs past `-t` and is SIGTERMed at about
355 s. The producer ignored the json-stream `error` event, so the run was
`completed=True` with `avg_gbps=0.0` (the stub's `sum_received`). The 31 s
closing interval was counted as a zero after peak and marked all 8 streams
as affected.

## Fix

- `scripts/iperf-json-metrics.py`: `stream_client_error()` records the
  json-stream `error` event as `client_error`, and such a run is
  `completed=false`. `avg_gbps` is the time-weighted mean of the measured
  intervals (`avg_source=intervals`). Intervals that end more than 1 s past
  the configured duration are excluded only when the client was interrupted
  (`post_duration_intervals_excluded`). A completed run is unchanged.
- `test/incus/iperf-throughput-lib.sh`: the `iperf_client_interrupt_verdict`
  cell prints exactly one PASS/FAIL/VOID line. It keeps an interrupted run
  red, now under the right name.
- `test-failover.sh` and `ha-smoke-iperf-lib.sh` call the cell. The ha-smoke
  path maps VOID to FAIL because its callers accept only PASS/FAIL. Its
  metrics-unavailable branch emits the cell too, so there is one cell per
  class.

Real logs, old → new producer. The loss rows used `--event-sec 50 --event-sec 200`; the tcp rows used the crash and failback seconds read from each log:

| run | avg_gbps | completed | collapse | affected streams |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1481034` loss (completed) | 9.78 → 9.78 | T → T | F → F | 6 → 6 |
| `2139278` loss (completed) | 14.68 → 14.68 | T → T | F → F | 8 → 8 |
| `1479181` tcp (interrupted) | 0.0 → 25.9 | T → F | T → T (the real crash zeros remain) | 8 → 4 (4 truly dead) |
| `1902225` tcp (interrupted) | 0.0 → 30.53 | T → F | T → F | 8 → 8 (event windows approximated) |

  replacement. 12 of 13 were KILLED: producer reverted to origin/master,
  `completed` ignoring the interrupt, no post-duration exclusion, avg left on
  the stub, error event not detected, slack widened to 60 s, helper always

## Risk

- `test-rolling-upgrade.sh` reads `avg_gbps` only when `completed` is true.
  An interrupted rolling-upgrade client therefore turns from a false
  "throughput 0.0" FAIL into a VOID "incomplete". Its per-stream oracle
  still judges the streams.
- `scripts/userspace-*.sh` consumers see `completed=false` and a real
  `avg_gbps` for an interrupted client, where they used to see
  `completed=true` and 0.0.
