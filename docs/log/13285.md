## Why

`ScreenState` accumulated fail-closed substituted-profile drops, SYN-flood destination/source splits and alarm crossings, permitted `alarm-without-drop` matches, profile warnings, and scan/sweep tracker pressure. Most accessors were test-only or dead-code-allowed, so worker status and Prometheus discarded the operational/security signals. In particular, an operator could not distinguish audit-mode permits or detector-table saturation from a quiet screen.

## What

- Made the nine `ScreenState` counter accessors available in production and
  publish their cumulative values on the first live binding at the existing
  one-second telemetry tick.
- Carried the worker-local totals through `BindingLiveState`, its snapshot,
  coordinator binding status, the Rust/Go `BindingStatus` wire fields, and the
  checked-in wire specimen.
- Added process-wide Prometheus counters for substituted-default drops,
  SYN-flood source/destination drops and alarms, alarm-without-drop permits,
  missing/inert profile warnings, and scan/sweep tracker skips/evictions. The
  Rust worker-local counters use one live binding as a transport slot; Go sums
  the slots rather than presenting false per-binding attribution.
- Added Rust snapshot and wire-round-trip coverage plus a Go metric aggregation
  regression.

## Risk

Metrics are worker/process-level, not per-interface or per-zone attribution; the existing `screen_reason_drops` family remains the per-reason drop attribution surface. Values are sampled on the existing one-second worker telemetry cadence. Older helpers omit the additive fields, which decode as zero on Go's status struct.
