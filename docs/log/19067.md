**Mutants of the guard.** Cycle 1 corrected this section: the cycle-0 text
called M3 a coverage gap, which was wrong (review R13-2, R8-F1).

The lock and writer census, at the guard in `stopClusterCommsWithFinalizer`:
- `clusterCommsGen` has exactly two writers:
  - `beginClusterCommsEpoch`, which bumps it under `clusterCommsLifecycleMu`;
  - the stop's own increment, serialized against other stops by
    `clusterCommsStopMu`.
- `d.sessionSync` becomes non-nil only in `publishSessionSyncIfCurrent`, and
  only when `gen == clusterCommsGen`. Every other write stores nil.
- The stop holds `clusterCommsLifecycleMu` from its re-capture through the guard
  and the finalizer. Every early return in between unlocks first.
- A begin that lands between the stop's increment and that lock is caught by
  the re-capture, which returns false before the clear.

So whenever the guard is reached, the generation is `stopGen` and
`d.sessionSync` is nil. The guard is a defensive assertion that is invariantly
true under `clusterCommsLifecycleMu`.

The equivalence covers ONLY that final predicate (Codex F1, cycle 2). The
earlier checks are not redundant:
- the re-capture after taking the lock (generation plus session identity);
- `stopStillCurrent` between the subsystem resets;
- the generation check under `clusterCommsMu` before the clear;
- `clusterCommsStopMu`;
- the lifecycle lock in `beginClusterCommsEpoch`.

Each guards a state that IS reachable. For example, a begin that lands between
the stop's increment and its lock is caught by the re-capture, and M5 shows
the lock is load-bearing.
