# #18622 — refuse synced topology changes before promotion

- **Branch:** `fix/18622-drive`, based on master `ce71b75385`.
- **Files:** `pkg/daemon/daemon_apply_commit.go`, `pkg/daemon/peer_sync_control_endpoint_refusal_17030_test.go`, `pkg/daemon/peer_sync_pre_promotion_refusal_18622_test.go`, `pkg/daemon/README.md`, `docs/ha-no-hitless-restart.md`, `docs/log/18622.md`.

## Why

`syncAndApplyWithAncestry` promoted peer text with `Store.SyncApply` before its #8965 peer-address, #8987 control-interface and #9121 config-sync-endpoint refusal checks. A refusal therefore left the refused topology active; a background applier could subsequently re-apply it without preflight and restart cluster communications onto the unreachable endpoint. Re-pushes also stopped reaching an effective refusal once the changed config was active.

## What

All three checks now execute in order inside the existing `Store.SyncApplyGated` pre-promotion gate, after the #18606 local-address check and before active promotion. Their post-promotion branches are removed. A refusal leaves active config, persistence and rollback history unchanged; identical retries and later changed-content pushes carrying the refused transport change are refused again. The #5840 topology and #6192 identity backstops remain post-promotion and unchanged.
**Modularity audit:** `daemon_apply_commit.go` grows from 1495 to 1526 LOC and crosses the 1500 floor. The `[WATCH]` entry in `docs/refactoring-audit-accepted.txt` records the decision to keep this candidate's gate and promotion ordering together with the sync-and-apply path rather than split the correctness boundary across files.

**#6720 authorization contract:** unlike those two remaining promote-then-refuse backstops, a refused #8965/#8987/#9121 candidate never becomes active policy. Its authorization changes (including web-management credential rotation/revocation) are neither promoted nor reconciled; the listener continues using authorization from the still-active config. A later accepted push or local commit promotes the authorization change, and the ordinary apply reconciles it. This is intentional: applying auth from a rejected candidate would split authorization from the config this node refused to converge on, while promoting it would restore the topology that the refusal is meant to prevent. The #17030 regression cell now pins this behavior on a live management listener; the #18622 refusal fixtures also carry an auth rotation and assert unchanged active config/background-applied config.

## Risk

A standby that nacks retains its prior active configuration and authorization. Any credential revocation carried only by the refused text remains unapplied on that standby, visible to the sender as the NACK, until an accepted correction converges the nodes. That is the fail-closed outcome: no background applier can adopt the refused topology. The actual cluster still requires the documented both-nodes/offline procedure for these transport moves.
