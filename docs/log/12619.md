## Why

The test asserted that the t=60s refill always ended at `flap-07`. That
assumed the initial window always left the budget cursor at policy order 4.
The worker consumes concurrently with `HandleEvent` admission, and the queue's
supersede/refill path allows the worker to dequeue while the batch is being
built. The initial window's actual last commit can therefore differ by
scheduler timing; the cursor correctly continues from that committed policy.
A later refill through `flap-06` is valid when that cursor begins at order 3,
so the unconditional assertion was flaky rather than evidence of a production
budget violation.

## What

The test parks the worker with the existing publication gate while each tested
batch is admitted, reopening it after the worker dequeues the batch's first
action and the producer finishes. At t=60s, it snapshots the protected live
budget cursor and asserts the final committed policy is the fourth successor
in rotation. This verifies the fairness contract independent of which policy
won the initial scheduling race. Commit-count, drop-count, boundary, and
operator-latency assertions remain unchanged.
