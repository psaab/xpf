# Issue #12174 — re-stop and re-snapshot a STOPPED resume

## Why

A durable `STOPPED` journal entry did not prove the daemon stayed down across an interruption. If the old unit restarted before a same-target resume, the resume skipped both STOP and the cut-boundary DB snapshot, then FLIPed under the live old daemon. The version-bound health check rejected that old helper, and auto-rollback restored the stale pre-restart snapshot over commits made after restart.

## What

On a resume at `STOPPED`, the runner now resolves `versions/current` before FLIP. If it still names the recorded previous version, the runner stops the unit again, takes a fresh config-DB snapshot, and durably updates the journal before continuing. If `current` already names the target, the resumed FLIP is stopped/replayed without replacing the pre-upgrade snapshot, which may be needed if the target already migrated the DB. An unexpected current version refuses the flip. A snapshot/persist failure restarts the old daemon and returns without proceeding to FLIP.

Added an interrupted-STOPPED, old-unit-restarted, same-target fixture with a distinguishable commit before resume and at the STOP boundary. Its forced target-health failure exercises auto-rollback and verifies that the fresh snapshot preserves the commit. Stopped-unit and different-staged-version controls pin the existing paths.
