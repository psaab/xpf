# 9955 — overlapping leak resolution: the differential, and why the proposed fix is not sufficient

## What was asked

The acceptance asked for **a differential over a generated set of overlap
shapes, not a single hand-built case**, and named the fix point: *"the
`routes.go` boundary is where the ordering information is dropped, so that is
where it is either carried through or the overlap is refused."*

The differential is built and lands with this change
(`userspace-dp/src/afxdp/forwarding/tests_leak_overlap_9955.rs`). It drives the
**real** `build_forwarding_state` + `lookup_forwarding_resolution` rather than a
re-implementation of the helper's rule, because a re-implementation would agree
with itself and prove nothing.

**The measurement says the named fix is necessary but NOT sufficient.** Details
below; the scope decision is the lead's, which is why no resolution change is
made here.

## Reachability, confirmed first

- The two resolvers genuinely use different algorithms, by construction.
  `pkg/routing/rules.go` sets `rule.Priority = prio + added`, monotonically
  increasing, and kernel FIB rules are evaluated in priority order with first
  match winning — prefix length is not a tiebreak. `fib.rs::sort_routes` orders
  each table by descending prefix length then ascending preference, and its own
  comment states the intent: the most specific, "NOT the insertion-order first".
- The ordering key never crosses the boundary. In
  `pkg/dataplane/userspace/routes.go` the only uses of `Priority` are the PBR
  window classification (31000-31999); `RouteSnapshot` carries no priority field
  at all.
- No commit-time gate: a search for an overlap gate over leak / `next-table` /
  rib-group rules in `pkg/config` returns nothing. The configuration is accepted
  silently, which is the reachability claim as filed.

## What the differential measures

### Shape family 1 — two overlapping leaks. 5 of 10 shapes diverge.

| arrangement | kernel | helper |
|---|---|---|
| broad next-table (prio 150) + specific rib-group (prio 30500) | next-table | **rib-group** |
| broad rib-group (prio 30500) + specific next-table (prio 150) | next-table | next-table (agrees) |

Five prefix-length pairs × two priority arrangements. Every shape in the first
arrangement diverges; every shape in the second agrees, because there the more
specific prefix *also* carries the better priority and both algorithms rank the
same entry first.

**That 50/50 split is exactly why a generated set was required.** A single
hand-built case drawn from the agreeing half would have shown no defect at all,
and one drawn from the diverging half would not have revealed that half the
space agrees by luck. Neither would have been safe to act on.

### Shape family 2 — a leak overlapping an ordinary route in the same table.

A /8 leak and a /24 route in `inet.0`. The helper prefers the /24 route. In the
kernel these are not peers: the leak is an ip RULE at priority 150 and the main
table is only reached through `from all lookup main` at 32766, so the rule fires
first regardless of prefix length and the packet is redirected into the target
VRF.

### Shape family 3 — a leak into a table that MISSES. This is the decisive one.

Kernel FIB rules **fall through** on a table miss: a matching rule sends the
lookup to its table, and if that table has no route, evaluation continues with
the next rule and eventually reaches main. So a leak pointing at a table that
cannot route the packet costs nothing.

The helper cannot do this. `lookup_forwarding_resolution_v4_inner` ends the leak
arm with `return lookup_forwarding_resolution_v4_inner(...)` — the recursive
result is returned **unconditionally**, so a target-table miss becomes the final
answer and the packet is blackholed where the kernel forwards it.

## The finding: one modelling error, three symptoms

All three divergences follow from a single cause. The kernel mechanism is
**two-stage** — priority-ordered rules *with fall-through*, then per-table
longest-prefix-match — and the helper models it as **one sorted list per table**
with unconditional recursion.

This is why "carry the ordering key through" does not close it, and the reason is
structural rather than a matter of effort:

- No choice of comparator over a single list reproduces a two-stage mechanism
  with fall-through. Fall-through is not an ordering property at all; it is a
  *retry* on miss, and a sorted list has nowhere to express it.
- Worse, ordering leaks by priority *within* a prefix-length-sorted list is not
  even well defined on its own. To place a leak relative to an ordinary route you
  must first decide whether leaks precede routes — which is shape family 2, a
  wider behaviour change than this issue scoped.

So adding a `rule_priority` field and re-sorting would close shape family 1 only
by silently taking a position on shape family 2, and would leave shape family 3
untouched. That is the shape of fix that my own notes warn about: patching one
symptom of a modelling error and calling the class closed.

## Status and what is proposed

**Landing here: the differential only.** All five cells pass as
*characterizations* of current behaviour, each documenting the direction it pins
and what it should become when the model is fixed. A cell that survives the fix
unchanged was not pinning the behaviour the fix changes, so these are written to
flip.

No resolution change is made, because the honest options are both wider than the
issue as filed:

1. **Model the two stages.** Hold leaks as a priority-ordered list consulted
   before the table's LPM, and continue past a target-table miss instead of
   returning it. Faithful, and a real change to the forwarding path with its own
   gate requirements.
2. **Refuse the overlap at commit.** Cheaper and fails closed, but it rejects
   configurations the kernel accepts today and has the repo-wide blast radius any
   new commit gate has.

Two fixture faults were caught during construction and are worth recording,
because each would have produced a confident wrong answer:

- **The single-leak control fired first.** Before neighbours were added to the
  fixture, nothing resolved and the differential reported *10 of 10 shapes
  diverge*. That reads as a dramatic finding and was entirely an artifact — the
  control exists to separate "the helper chose differently" from "the helper
  chose nothing", and it is the only reason the number was not believed.
- **The fall-through cell could not detect its own defect.** Its fallback route
  was more specific than the leak, so the prefix-length sort returned the
  fallback without ever entering the leak arm; the cell reported a forward
  candidate that said nothing about fall-through. Inverting the prefixes so the
  leak is the more specific entry is what makes the leak arm reachable.
