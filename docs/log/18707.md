# #18707: helper restart waits on fixed delays before forwarding

- **Timestamp**: 2026-11-01
- **Action**: Measured where an isolated helper crash spends its outage, then
  removed the three fixed delays on that path.
- **Prior evidence** (#18702's live cell, udp, 70,526 spawn-flushed rows,
  journal `short-precise`, VM clock; the 1.6 s probe could not split it):

  | event | t (s, from exit) |
  |---|---|
  | exit detected, restart scheduled `attempt=1 delay=1s` | 0.000 |
  | spawn (`event stream: listening`) | +1.001 |
  | spawn flush done (70,526 rows) | +1.072 |
  | `helper started` | +1.174 |
  | `apply_snapshot` / `set_forwarding_state armed=true` (first status tick) | +2.180 |
  | worker binds | +2.20 .. +2.99 |
  | ctrl prewarm + `starting XSK liveness probe` (ctrl=1) | +2.991 |
  | `bootstrapping NAPI queues reason=startup-prewarm` | +3.062 |
  | `XSK liveness proven` | +5.177 |
  | `RG readiness: ready` | +5.563 |

  The spawn-time 3 s NAPI timer would have fired at about +4.2 and was
  swallowed by the prewarm's 2 s debounce, so it was not on the critical
  path in this run. The 1 s backoff and the 1 s wait for the first status
  tick were.
- **Changes**:
  - First retry of a crash episode is immediate
    (`helperRestartDelay(1) == 0`); repeats keep the exponential schedule.
    Cumulative wait before the crash-loop cap is unchanged (63 s), so HA's
    `CrashLooping` response is not reached sooner; it trips at attempt 8
    instead of 7.
  - The crash restart's status loop runs its first pass immediately
    (`ensureStatusLoopFirstPassNowLocked`).
  - The spawn-time 3 s NAPI timer is replaced by a once-per-generation,
    non-debounced bootstrap fired when the status reports every binding
    bound (`ensureXSKBoundNAPIBootstrapLocked`), with the #10436 fence.
- **File(s)**: `pkg/dataplane/userspace/{process_supervisor.go,process_status.go,process.go,helper_status_apply.go,manager.go}`,
  tests (`helper_restart_latency_18707_test.go`, the ported
  `process_napi_startup_10436_test.go`, three contract updates, `statusLoop`
  call sites), `pkg/dataplane/README.md`,
  `docs/userspace-dataplane-architecture.md`, `docs/in-place-upgrade.md`.

- **Reading**:
  - **An isolated crash (t1) cut transit loss from 3.48 s to 1.62 s after
    SIGKILL**, which is 1.08 s after the supervisor saw the exit.
  - Repeats still back off: after t2 and t3 are each one backoff step
    shorter than before (1 s and 2 s instead of 2 s and 4 s). The loss
    shrinks by exactly that step plus the 1 s tick.
  - The remaining ~1.6 s is work, not waiting:
    - about 0.5 s for the kernel to tear down the dead helper (SIGKILL
      to reaped exit, the same in both builds);
    - about 0.2 s for spawn, flush and ready;
    - about 0.7 s for the helper's 18 binds and fill primes.

  first forwarded ping came before `XSK liveness proven`. Ctrl is armed

  +1.737 and +2.693, each 1.00 s after loop start (+0.733 and +1.690). It
  gates `RG readiness`
  (takeover readiness, about 1.5 s later still), not forwarding. So it was
  left on the 1 s cadence, and the transit target is met without it.

    manual failback is refused on fw1 with `demotion peer barrier failed:

    `144aeb581f37754d`.

    follow-up lock cell restored node0 primary for all RGs and re-applied

- **PR trial 1 above lost its immediate first pass.** `helper started`
  +0.721 was followed by `apply_snapshot` only at +1.726, a full tick
  later; in every other after-trial the gap was 5 to 9 ms. That is
  consistent with the #16653 stale-poll fence discarding the immediate
  pass, because a concurrent status apply (the daemon's own reaction to
  the restart) bumped `lastStatusSeq` while the poll was off-lock. The
  discard is Debug-level, so the journal cannot show it. Fold: until one
  pass runs to the end, a discarded first pass is retried after 100 ms, at
  most 10 times (`statusLoopFirstPassRetry` / `statusLoopFirstPassRetries`).
  Then the ticker is reset so the next pass is a full interval away. The
  loop now reads its seams once, at start.

## Re-measure after merging #18704 (merge head `76e18fd393`, udp `0.0.29586+g76e18fd393f4`)

- **Question.** #18704 (137c6a2cfa) raises election debt on any unexpected
  helper exit (#18697) and holds it until the next helper is armed and
  healthy. Does an isolated crash on the RG primary still avoid a needless
  RG move, or does it cause one cleanly?
- **Method.** Same burst, SIGKILL and 10 Hz ping as above, plus:
  - an `iperf3 -P4 -t34 -i0.2` TCP run through the RG owner;
  - in-VM RG samplers on both nodes, about 0.3 s apart;
  - both nodes' journals, with fw1 events shifted by the measured
    fw1-minus-fw0 offset.

  Trials are 130 s apart and node0 is restored between them
  (`/var/tmp/fz/r-18707-move-cell.sh`, `r-18707-move-analyze.py`).
- **Valid samples.** Three crashes on the RG primary: `pr-B4` t1, and
  `pr-B4-hp` t1 and t3. In the other trials a spontaneous wedge verdict
  (#18780, below) had already moved the data RGs to fw1, so the crash hit a
  node holding only RG0. Those cost 1–3 ping probes and no TCP stall.

  | step (s after SIGKILL) | pr-B4 t1 | pr-B4-hp t1 | pr-B4-hp t3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | exit observed | +0.508 | +0.472 | +0.498 |
  | fw0 debt held for the new generation (#18697) | +0.599 | +0.590 | +0.587 |
  | fw0 helper ready | +0.700 | +0.691 | +0.689 |
  | fw0 demotes RG1/RG2 (RG0 stays) | +0.707 | +0.698 | +0.701 |
  | fw1 primary transition | +0.793 | +0.887 | +0.934 |
  | fw1 steering republish done (first RG, ~28–33k rows) | +1.483 | +1.714 | +1.698 |
  | fw1 VIPs added, GARP | +1.491 | +1.724 | +1.709 (RG2; RG1's republish follows) |
  | fw0 ctrl=1 (its own helper forwarding-ready) | +1.517 | +1.186 | +1.179 |
  | **first forwarded ping (via fw1)** | **+1.824** | **+2.179** | **+2.929** |
  | ping lost at 10 Hz | 17 | 21 | 28 |
  | TCP: zero-throughput 0.2 s intervals, resets | 14 (~2.8 s), 0 | 15 (~3.0 s), 0 | 15 (~3.0 s), 0 |
  | fw0 debt cleared | +7.607 | +7.278 | +8.097 |
  | RGs move back | no | no | no |

- **Reading.**
  - **The crash causes an RG move, every time.** #18697's debt is held 0.09 s
    after the exit, before the replacement helper is even ready, so the
    faster restart cannot pre-empt it. fw0's own helper is forwarding-ready
    at +1.2–1.5 s, by which point the data RGs are already on fw1.
  - **The move is clean but not hitless.** There is one contiguous outage of
    1.8–2.9 s:
    - no second gap when fw0's helper comes back or when its debt clears;
    - no move back; RG0 stays on fw0;
    - every TCP stream survives a ~3 s stall (RTO backoff on the 1.8–2.9 s
      loss) with zero resets.
  - **What sets the outage is fw1's takeover, not fw0's restart.** fw1
    republishes ~30k steering rows per activated RG in 0.7–0.8 s before it
    adds that RG's VIPs. In t3 the two data RGs activated one after the
    other, which is why t3 is the slowest.
  - **Against a local-only recovery.** The isolated trials above (no #18704)
    recovered in 1.6–1.8 s. The move therefore costs about the same to
    ~1.2 s more, and leaves the RGs on the peer.
  - **Inference, not measured.** Master + #18704 without #18717 would make
    the same move at the same time, because the debt is keyed on the exit,
    not on the restart. What differs is how long fw0's own helper stays down
    (about 1 s backoff plus 1 s tick longer) and when its debt clears. A
    paired master control was not run: the shared heavy-cargo queue never
    built it.
- **Whether a quick restart should keep the RGs at all** is #18704's design
  call, not this PR's. One option: hold the unexpected-exit debt only if the
  replacement is not armed within a short grace (about 2 s), so a 1.6 s
  local recovery would not trade itself for a 1.8–2.9 s takeover.
- **Found along the way:**
  - **#18780.** A worker stalls about 11 s, 1.5–4 min after a 17k-session
    burst. It happens on a fresh helper with no crash, too. #18704's
    watchdog moves the RGs, and that worker's flows black-hole about 24 s.
    This is what moved the RGs between trials.
  - **udp hugepages.** fw0's 2 MB hugepage pool had been 0 since its
    test-failover crash reboots, because the deb ships no hugepage sysctl
    (#16455). The helper's UMEM ran in standard pages (RSS up to 5.7 GB) and
    was OOM-killed under burst load at 20:49:41. Restored to 1440 pages and
    persisted in `/etc/sysctl.d/99-xpf-hugepages.conf` on both udp VMs.
    `pr-B4` t1 ran without the pool, `pr-B4-hp` with it.
