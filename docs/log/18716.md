    - In legacy VRRP mode (`no-private-rg-election` without
      `no-reth-vrrp`) the #18701/#18715 helper-down arm was gated off
      (`noRethVRRP &&`). The RETH VRRP resign that releases the VIPs ran
      after the 5 s prep, and the rg_active write was unbounded.
    - Narrowed analysis: on every RG move the new primary forces MASTER
      at priority 200 while the old one is still MASTER at 200. The
      IPv4-anchored tie-break (#4376) then gives one of three outcomes:
      no window; the VIP stays on the old (wedged) node; or, with no
      non-VIP IPv4 on the member, both stay MASTER (dual VIP).
  - **Action**: fix, branch `fix/18716-legacy-resign-first` on master
    b725d9542e.
    - `handleClusterEventMerged`: `helperDown` no longer depends on the
      mode.
      - Legacy mode, on the primary to secondary or secondary-hold edge
        (the old resign's edge): `resignLostRethRGsHelperDown` resigns
        the event RG FIRST. It also resigns every sibling with
        cluster non-primary and any VRRP MASTER, which is the reconcile
        posture net's predicate without its 2 s delay. Nothing extra is
        resigned during VRRP sync hold.
      - The prep is then skipped when the control socket is silent, and
        the write is bounded, as in direct mode.
      - A healthy helper keeps prep, resign, write.
    - Fence: a failed bounded write with control silent at entry and
      silent now (`legacyHelperDownFenceSilentAck`) resolves from the
      RETH VIP release (`awaitRethVIPRelease`), not from the write. A
      failed release, or an answering helper's refusal, still fails it.
    - `reconcileRGState`: the prep skip and the bounded write apply in
      legacy mode too. No early resign there, because the posture net's
      2 s delay still guards the peer-loss transient.
    - Residual, stated: on the election path both nodes act on their own
      events, so the dual-MASTER interval (shapes 1 and 3) runs from the
      peer's force to this node's resign. It is no longer stretched by
      helper timeouts, but it is not zero. The peer-requested path has
      no window: the peer promotes on the ack, and the ack follows the
      release.
    - Not measured: which tie-break shape a real legacy deployment is
      in. The lab clusters run the compiler default.
