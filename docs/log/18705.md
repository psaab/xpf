## (A) Stamp provenance, and whether the refresh's heal can be lost

- **Timestamp**: 2026-11-16 09:15 UTC
  - **Action**: Analysis at c61aa074f0. The verdict is that the heal-loss
    interleaving is real at master and nothing covers it.

    The coordinator's order:
    - `ha/state.rs:78` bumps `rg_epochs[0]` (Release), under `ha_mutex`.
    - `:81` stores `rg_runtime`.
    - `:82` drops the mutex.
    - The push comes later and outside the mutex: the debug `eprintln` at
      `:84-86`, the activation `eprintln!` plus
      `lock_shared_recover(..).len()` at `:167-176`, then
      `handle_activated_rgs` (`:177`). That pushes `RefreshOwnerRGS` per
      live worker at `:249`. A refused push becomes debt at `:256-264`.
      So store-to-push includes at least one stderr write: microseconds.

    The worker's order (`worker/loop_body/mod.rs`):
    - `:1072` `ha_runtime = ha_state.load()`;
    - `:1078` `has_commands` (try_lock, peek only);
    - `:1086` debt epochs;
    - `:1098` `apply_worker_commands(.., ha_runtime.as_ref() (:1105), ..)`.

    The DRAIN is inside the apply. It happens at
    `session_glue/worker_commands.rs:193`
    (`commands::take_worker_command_slice`), after the view load. The debt
    take is at `:239`, after the slice dispatch.

    So the interleaving is:
    1. The worker loads the OLD view (`:1072`).
    2. The coordinator bumps, stores and pushes.
    3. The worker drains `RefreshOwnerRGS` and dispatches it under the OLD
       view.

    This needs `has_commands` to be true for some other reason (bulk-sync
    imports in a failover), or a debt epoch change. It also needs the
    worker to be held between `:1072` and the drain for longer than the
    coordinator's store-to-push (preemption or vCPU steal). It is rare but
    possible.

    Under the old view, the refresh's re-resolution gives `HAInactive`
    for RG1 egresses. The rewrite gate excludes that
    (`session_glue/commands/refresh_owner_rgs.rs:75-80`), so nothing is
    rewritten. The command is consumed. Its debt twin is worse: the
    `rg_is_active` filter (`worker_commands.rs:355`) stale-skips it and
    consumes it.

    Nothing re-runs the refresh. A later `update_ha_state` diffs against
    the stored state and finds no activation. The lease fast path
    (`ha/session_domain.rs:1637`) never pushes commands. The hit path
    re-resolves live per packet for forwarding (`resolve.rs:686-760`), but
    it persists the forward row's line only when the FIB generation is
    stale (`resolve.rs:849`). A reverse hit persists only into the reverse
    row. So the forward row's stored line stays at the split-FIB reth0.50
    until a forward slow-path hit after a FIB bump.

    Consequence for the brief's stamp options:
    - Reading `rg_epoch_reader(0)` at refresh time would stamp the table
      "refreshed" over a stale line. That is the #18705 revocation, and it
      would be permanent.
    - A second activation, k+1, bumping between the view load and the
      stamp would make that reader value too new.
    - The same interleaving also loses a `DemoteOwnerRGS`. The old view
      still shows the RG active, and a demote debt is stale-skipped.

    Same mechanism, fixed here:
    - **View after drain.** A new `session_glue/command_phase.rs`
      `run_worker_command_phase` does the queue check and the debt
      epochs, then the apply. It loads no view first. The tick
      (`apply_worker_commands_with_view`) calls a view source after the
      drain and again after the debt take. The queue (debt) mutex orders
      the coordinator's store before that load.
    - **Published epoch.** `HaState::published_epoch0` is new.
      `update_ha_state` stores `rg_epochs[0]` into it (Release) after the
      `rg_runtime` store, still under `ha_mutex`. The view source reads
      it (Acquire) just before `load_full()`, so the view shows at least
      that epoch's state.
    - **Stamp.** A `RefreshOwnerRGS` dispatch stamps that published value
      on the table (`note_owner_rgs_refreshed`, monotone, wrap-safe). As
      first committed, that covered the queued arm
      (`worker_command_helpers.rs`, INCLUDING an RG0-only command) and the
      debt arm when any RG survived the filter. Fold 1 (K2, below) drops
      the RG0-only stamp: it was wrong.

    This is shape (ii): the epoch comes from the view actually used. Shape
    (i) was rejected. It changes the `WorkerCommand` and debt formats (57
    references), and on its own it does not fix the heal loss, because
    the queue check is a peek and the drain is a later lock.

    **CORRECTED in fold 1** (r24 P2-1, Codex P1-2/P1-3, r23 P2-1). This
    entry first claimed "the stamp can only be too OLD … and never reopens
    the window". That was FALSE as written. The published value is a lower
    bound for the HA VIEW, but the stamp also claims the rows were
    re-resolved under that view against a current FIB. Two dispatches broke
    that claim:
    - an RG0-only refresh re-resolved nothing and stamped a view that
      could already show a later RG1 activation, a too-NEW stamp (K2);
    - a refresh resolved against the pass's tick-start FIB, which could
      predate the activation's connected-route republish (K3).

    What holds after fold 1: the stamp is credited only by a refresh that
    ran its full scan (an RG > 0), under an HA view loaded after the drain,
    against a forwarding loaded after that view. The coordinator publishes
    the activation's FIB before storing the activation (#16270, both under
    the server lock). So the stamp is at most the epoch of activations
    whose rows the dispatch re-resolved against their FIB; any error is
    toward too old, which lengthens the bounded keep.

## (B) Does every epoch0 bump reach every worker's stamp?

- **Timestamp**: 2026-11-16 09:15 UTC
  - **Action**: Analysis at c61aa074f0. The verdict is no. A bound is
    added, and every path that can be closed is closed.

    `rg_epochs[0]` has exactly one writer, `ha/state.rs:78`, and it bumps
    only on activation. `try_refresh_ha_leases` never publishes an
    activation (#16382, `ha/session_domain.rs:1797-1818`).

    Paths that deliver a stamp:
    - A queued push (`:249`), drained at the next pass.
    - A refused push. It becomes debt (`:256-264`, `worker_queue.rs:284`)
      and is replayed at its countdown, which is at most 4096/256 = 16
      passes, or when the queue empties. It stamps when any RG survives
      the filter.
    - A poisoned queue: `lock_recover` and `try_lock_recover` recover and
      clear the poison (#1807), so the command is not lost.
    - Two activations close together: each push stamps the published
      value at its own dispatch, monotone max. Debt merges by
      supersession (`worker_queue.rs:268-276`), and the surviving refresh
      stamps a value at least as new.
    - Dead workers are skipped (`ha/state.rs:238`), but a dead worker
      polls nothing. Its successor gets a fresh table, seeded at the
      published epoch in `worker_loop`.

    Paths that deliver NO stamp:
    1. A debt refresh superseded at record time by a later demote of
       every RG it named (`supersede`, `worker_queue.rs:268-276`).
    2. A debt refresh whose RGs were all demoted again before it replayed.
       It is stale-skipped (`worker_commands.rs:355`). Stamping there
       would claim rows that were never re-resolved, so it does not stamp.
    3. A worker whose record is published after `handle_activated_rgs`
       takes its records snapshot (`:233`). The worker thread starts
       before its record publishes (`worker_queue.rs:486-489`, review B6).
       Its seed reads the published epoch, which can lag a bump in flight.

    On those paths the stamp would lag forever, until the next activation.
    That would make reverse-hit SNAT revocation of local-companion rows a
    standing exemption: a fail-open.

    **Bound chosen.** `OWNER_RG_REFRESH_PENDING_KEEP_NS` = 1 s, counted
    from the table's FIRST decline for that epoch value
    (`SessionTable::owner_rg_refresh_pending`).

    Justification. A DELIVERED refresh lands within 16 passes. Each pass
    is bounded by the 256-command slice (~256 us measured, #7201) plus
    one RX batch. `worker_queue.rs` pins at build time that the bound is
    at least 6 x 16 passes x 10 ms. Past the bound, the judgment runs as
    before #18705.
