## Verdict discipline

Three distinctions are load-bearing and each one changed at least one verdict:

1. **Mechanism and consequence are separate verdicts.** Four of the ten rows
   have opposite answers on the two axes. Collapsing them either way closes a
   real defect on a technicality or manufactures one from correct code.
2. **Fixed-since is not wrong-when-written.** Four rows were correct at their
   base and are closed by commits that postdate it; one (`009`) was wrong at its
   own base because the mitigation it asks for was already in the tree, one call
   site upstream of the code it quoted. Filing those together misrepresents the
   reviewer in both directions.
3. **Not reached is not dismissed.** Where the answer needs a measurement that
   was not run, the row says so and names the measurement.

## Carried forward

- **`036`** is the one actionable defect. A static-NAT `match destination-port`
  token that fails to parse leaves `MatchDestinationPort = 0`, which every layer
  down to the Rust matcher defines as "match any port", so an intended
  port-forward becomes whole-address 1:1. It emits **no** warning, because the
  warning's predicate is `MatchDestinationPort != 0` — the field the parse
  failure zeroes. Reachable on the lenient / peer-sync path only. The fix exists
  one axis over: destination-NAT preserves raw unparsed tokens in
  `NATMatch.InvalidDestinationPorts` so the strict gate can reject and the
  snapshot builder can fail closed; static NAT has no such channel.
- **`071` residual**, with its expiry condition: a zone whose every netdev is
  excluded from `IngressNetdevs` (lifeline, zone-shared parent, or
  l3mdev-VRF-enslaved) emits no ingress-scoped rules and the bypass is live for
  it. The VRF exclusion is correct — `iifname` at `LOCAL_IN` names the master —
  so closing it means scoping on the master, not adding enslaved names.
- **`091` latent hazard**, with its expiry condition: the in-flight CAS is the
  only thing preventing the interleaving the finding describes. Anything that
  makes reconcile passes concurrent must re-derive `PrevAddr` after the lock is
  re-acquired rather than capturing it before the I/O.
- **`001` claim 2**, NOT REACHED: a declined WireGuard record does fall through
  to outer-datagram adjudication, but whether the resulting outer session is a
  defect needs the Rust suite plus a netns.

> ## The severity question, now EXECUTED: policy PERMITS it, bounded to `application any`
>
> Closing the half my earlier comment left open — *what does zone policy do with a
> frame classified as protocol 60 with zeroed ports?* Previously I read the
> matcher; this runs it. Measured at `06094a942` through `evaluate_policy`.
> **Research only; no code changed and `/engineer 8869` has not run.**
>
> ```
> application any,      proto=60 ports=0  ->  Permit
> application any,      proto=TCP         ->  Permit     <- control
> application junos-ssh, proto=60         ->  Deny
> ```
>
> Row 2 is the control: without it, a `Permit` on row 1 cannot be distinguished
> from a rule that permits nothing at all. Row 3 is the bound.
>
> ### The finding's consequence is CONFIRMED, and bounded
>
> On a zone pair permitted with **`application any`** — the ordinary permissive
> rule — a TCP segment prefixed with **eight or more IPv6 extension headers** is
> **forwarded**, having never been classified as TCP. No TCP-flag screen
> (`syn-fin`, `no-flag`, `fin-no-ack`, `syn-frag`) and no SYN-flood / SYN-cookie
> defence evaluated it: to every one of them `meta.protocol` is 60. The end host's
> stack parses the chain normally and receives the segment.
>
> A rule scoped to a **specific** application does not match protocol 60 and falls
> to the implicit default, so a tightly-scoped policy is not exposed. `application
> any` is the common case on a permissive pair, which is what makes this
> practically reachable rather than theoretical.
>
> ### A fixture artifact I caught before reporting it
>
> My first run of row 3 also said **Permit**, which would have made the exposure
> *every* permit rule rather than `application any` only — a much broader and
> wrong claim. The cause was my own fixture: `applications: vec!["junos-ssh"]`
> with `application_terms: Vec::new()`. With no terms to index, the compiled
> matcher has nothing to discriminate on and admits everything.
>
> Populating the term (`protocol tcp`, `destination-port 22`) flips row 3 to
> **Deny**. So the narrower claim in my previous comment was right, and the
> broader one never left the worktree.
>
> Recording it because the shape keeps recurring: **an under-populated fixture
> fails to a PERMISSIVE value here**, so the artifact and the defect look
> identical, and the artifact is the more alarming of the two.
>
