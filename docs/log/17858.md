# #17858 — the demotion sweep's ownership check preceded another blocking SA lookup, so failback could still lose the replacement SA

- **Timestamp**: 2026-10-27
  - **Action**: The #16820 guard in `terminateDemotedRGIPsecSAs`
    re-checked the RG ownership epoch and `!IsLocalPrimary(rg)` at the
    daemon loop boundary, then called `ipsecTerminate` →
    `ipsec.Manager.TerminateConnection`, which performs ANOTHER blocking
    `swanctl --list-sas` (resolving the gateway-named IKE connection,
    #15024) before the `swanctl --terminate --ike <conn> --force` exec.
    A sweep that passed the boundary check and stalled in that inner
    read survived a COMPLETE failback — epoch bump, takeover belt
    terminate, initiate — and its terminate then landed on the
    replacement SA, which carries the SAME IKE name; the final operation
    became terminate instead of initiate, recreating the #15194 failback
    black hole on the node that owns the RG (issue #17858, retro-review
    of #17614). Fixed by evaluating the SAME ownership predicate at BOTH
    decision points: `Manager.TerminateConnectionGuarded(name, guard)`
    runs the caller's guard AFTER the inner live-SA read, immediately
    before the terminate exec, while holding a new `Manager.opMu` — and
    `InitiateConnection` now holds that same mutex across its exec.
    Because every promotion edge bumps the epoch BEFORE launching
    `reinitiateIPsecSAsForRG` (the belt+initiate leg), a guard that
    reads the pre-failback epoch proves the failback initiate has not
    exec'd yet (an initiate completed under `opMu` would hand the mutex
    — and the epoch bump that strictly precedes it — down to the guard
    read), so the stale terminate is ordered BEFORE the initiate and can
    only hit the OLD SA; if failback already began, the guard reads the
    bumped epoch and aborts the whole sweep. The daemon sweep now calls
    `ipsecTerminateGuarded` (belt/operator paths keep plain
    `TerminateConnection` — the guard would always decline on the
    promotion side and break the belt). Added
    `ipsec_demote_sweep_inner_race_17858_test.go`: SUBJECT drives the
    REAL manager path (`d.ipsec` with a swanctl exec double;
    `ipsecTerminateFn` deliberately unset — a seam replacing the whole
    terminate cannot see inside it, which is how the #16820 suite
    stayed green over this window) with failback completing while the
    sweep is blocked in the terminate path's OWN `--list-sas`; it must
    end with `initiate:vpn-rg1` and exactly one (belt) terminate.
    CONTROL (no failback) must still issue the demotion terminate. Both
    cells JOIN the sweep instead of sleeping. Also keyed
    `twoRGIPsecStore` ahead of the #16746 control-link commit gate
    (`no-private-rg-election`, inert for these cells — RG state is
    driven via `SetGroupStateForTesting`, the config only feeds
    reth→RG attribution; precedent 68b1f8162a keyed fixtures ahead of
    #6611): without it the fixture — and every family built on it —
    fails at commit on current master, before any assertion runs.

  - **Risk**: `opMu` serializes the external effects of
    `InitiateConnection` and the terminate path process-wide, so an
    initiate waiting out its 5 s detach delay can hold a concurrent
    demotion sweep's terminate (and vice versa) for the duration of one
    swanctl exec — failover edges are rare and already shell out per
    SA, and the ordering is the point of the fix; no nesting exists
    (leaf execs only, `TerminateAllSAs`/`terminateIKE` keep their own
    direct `sc` calls). A guard abort leaves the demoted node's stale
    SA for the failback belt (unchanged #16820 behaviour). The wider
    unkeyed-fixture breakage on master is NOT addressed here — filed as
    its own issue.
