# #13643 — retry pressure-shed teardown after tuple contention

- **Timestamp**: 2026-10-13 (`fix/13643-campaign-zero`)

## Why

When the tuple-install lease was contended, pressure admission removed the selected victims from the local session table but did not schedule their shared, BPF, conntrack, or NAT state for a later cleanup. Their worker-owned SNAT/NAT64 holds and dataplane authority could therefore remain indefinitely.

## What

- Preserve pressure-shed victim metadata in a per-worker deferred queue when the all-key tuple lease cannot be acquired.
- Retry deferred teardown from periodic session housekeeping, not on the packet descriptor fast path. A successful retry takes one tuple lease over the queued keys, then removes matching shared authority, coordinator/BPF/conntrack/DNAT rows, flow-cache slots, local NAT holds, and peer replica state.
- Keep replacement rows intact when the shared-row identity predicate declines removal; release a worker's NAT hold only when there is no corresponding local replacement.
- Cover both the normal admission and guarded missing-neighbor admission cleanup paths, and extend the transactional regression to check deferred shared-row and SNAT-hold cleanup.

## Validation

- **Regression RED on pristine base**: the focused pressure-shed scenario failed on `761931ff6`; after lease contention, the worker SNAT pool retained 2 used ports instead of returning to 1 after a later cleanup attempt.
- **Focused GREEN**: `cargo test -p xpf-userspace-dp txn_pressure_shed_gate_failure_keeps_shared_row_and_nat_hold_12862 -- --nocapture` passed after the deferred retry implementation.
- **Affected module**: `cargo test -p xpf-userspace-dp afxdp::tests_txn_flow_cache:: -- --nocapture` passed (52 tests).
- **Formatting**: `cargo fmt --manifest-path Cargo.toml -- --check` is not clean; the formatter reports extensive repository-wide diffs outside this change. No whole-tree formatting was applied to avoid unrelated churn.

## Risk

Deferred victims remain charged and their dataplane state remains authoritative while tuple-gate contention persists; this intentionally avoids making their NAT allocations reusable before teardown can be fenced. Cleanup retries from periodic worker housekeeping, avoiding an additional potentially blocking lease acquisition in packet processing. The issue regression exercises release of the stale shared row and worker SNAT hold after contention clears.
