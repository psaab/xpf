# #9506 S5 q0 capture, provenance, and physical reinject commit

## Scope

S5 wires the userspace capture path end to end: strict IPv4/IPv6 payload
classification, `CaptureOrigin` registration and validation through the
slow-path/fragment pipeline, bounded per-flow handoff and fragment slots,
owner-homogeneous batching, and the Rust q0 full-frame writer with a physical
REINJECT ACK/commit point. The daemon stages queue generations before snapshot
publication, commits the nft diversion only after the matching dataplane
snapshot is fresh, and rolls back staged generations on failure.

The reinject authority announcement is persistent across submit-socket
reconnects. Rust keeps queue-epoch tombstones, transfers installed authority
under the shared target-swap lock, and treats the physical ACK as the commit
point; old-set conntrack ACK and the S4 permit/queue epoch contracts remain
in force.

## Snapshot protocol fence

The S4 authority fields are real transmitted `ConfigSnapshot` fields:
`permit_epoch` and `queue_epochs`. Their addition changed the shape digest
from `c098e2c1f595be54bd617f5ae0b2a2806ef8cfed34ce7cadad9c824d17b5c779` to
`a497626cd7021af40daaf16fac0335547ce677cc5f947a1dfeee688c06fb5b68`.
S5 therefore moved the Go and Rust snapshot protocol from 27 to 28 in
lockstep, refreshed the shape pin and the declared snapshot golden, and named
both fields in the version-history comments. An old helper must not accept a
snapshot while applying stale q0 ownership.

The Rust lifecycle retains a 1 ms `target_sync_thread` poll for this slice. It
samples `slow_path` under the server-state mutex and converges to the current
steady pointer within one poll interval; a transient A→B→A change may be
coalesced, but a superseded intermediate target is never published to
submitters and need not receive authority. Teardown shares the `running`/join
lifecycle. Replacing the poll with event-driven `ArcSwap` publication remains
follow-up cleanup, not an S5 correctness blocker.
