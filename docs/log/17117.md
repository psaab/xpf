## Change

- Extended the canonical `manualFailoverHoldSnapshot` into a single `operatorHoldSnapshot` (hold, override pair, aborted marker, generation, RG pointer) with `snapshotOperatorHoldLocked` / `restoreOperatorHoldLocked` in `failover.go`. The restore abandons when `failoverGen` has moved (a ResetFailover or in-flight ForceSecondary supersede), requires the same RG incarnation for the hold arm, leaves a newer hold untouched, and restores NOTHING when the snapshot's RG is absent or a different incarnation (fold: every arm is incarnation-gated).
- `handlePeerTimeout` now snapshots/restores through that pair; `peerLossHoldSnapshot15211` and its hand restore loop are deleted (`grep -n peerLossHoldSnapshot15211 pkg/cluster/*.go` is empty). Exactly one restore helper is called from both `abortRequestedPeerFailover{,Batch}` and `handlePeerTimeout`. The fence path forces `restoreWeight` so its historical unconditional pre-clear weight restore is preserved byte-for-byte.
- The restore writes the whole snapshotted `transferOverride{reqID, previous}` back directly (`failover.go`, `restoreOperatorHoldLocked` override arm) and stamps the live peer view inline (`StateSecondaryHold` + `StateOverriddenLocally`/`OverrideReason` #7367 fields + `rg.PeerPriority` refresh, which the old hand copy left stale); it deliberately does NOT re-apply through `applyPeerTransferOutOverrideLocked`, which would re-derive `previous` from the post-fence-wait peer view instead of restoring the snapshot (#17117-fold F1). The peer-transfer call sites clear the snapshot's override/aborted arms because their abort rolls the override back through its own reqID-matched `restorePeerTransferOutOverrideLocked`.
