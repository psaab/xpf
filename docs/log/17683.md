# #17683 — Release persistent NAT lease on recorded scope in pressure-shed and dropped-delete paths

- **Timestamp**: 2026-10-26
  - **Action**: Carried exact persistent-inbound lease ownership through pressure-shed teardown and same-key HA refreshes, including selected-owner migration; restricted missing-owner releases to the recorded allocator; separated ordinary NAT allocation repair from persistent inbound-hold release on queue-full peer deletes.
  - **Why**: Arrival-domain reconstruction could leave the granting allocator's lease held or decrement another tenant's overlapping lease. Pressure shedding could discard the exact hold, unchanged HA refreshes could increment a shared inbound lease without a matching release, and rule reordering could leave an existing hold recorded on the prior allocator. Queue-full repair must not consume the peer's recorded persistent hold before its delete-drop sweep does.
  - **What**: Added recorded-allocator-only release and a source-NAT repair variant that preserves persistent holds. Pressure-shed teardown intents now retain the exact hold, shared recovery preserves it, and unchanged HA refreshes reuse the installed hold rather than acquiring another; when HA refresh selects a different allocator for the same lease key, it releases the old exact owner and acquires one reference on the new owner. Shared-hit replicas acquire independent references; fenced WorkerLocalImport expiry releases only that replica's exact recorded hold while preserving serving cache/NAT state. Queue-full delete repair releases only the refusing worker's ordinary NAT/NAT64 reservation holders; its delete-drop sweep remains the sole owner of the recorded inbound hold. Added pressure, HA refresh/migration, missing-owner, shared-replica, expiry, and queue-full/sweep regressions with controls.
