# #17683 — Release persistent NAT lease on recorded scope in pressure-shed and dropped-delete paths

- **Timestamp**: 2026-10-26
  - **Action**: Carried exact persistent-inbound lease ownership through pressure-shed teardown and same-key HA refreshes; restricted missing-owner releases to the recorded allocator; separated ordinary NAT allocation repair from persistent inbound-hold release on queue-full peer deletes.
  - **Why**: Arrival-domain reconstruction could leave the granting allocator's lease held or decrement another tenant's overlapping lease. Pressure shedding could discard the exact hold, and unchanged HA refreshes could increment a shared inbound lease without a matching release. Queue-full repair must not consume the peer's recorded persistent hold before its delete-drop sweep does.
  - **What**: Added recorded-allocator-only release and a source-NAT repair variant that preserves persistent holds. Pressure-shed teardown intents now retain the exact hold, shared recovery preserves it, and unchanged HA refreshes reuse the installed hold rather than acquiring another. Queue-full delete repair now releases only the refusing worker's ordinary NAT/NAT64 reservation holders; the worker's delete-drop sweep remains the sole owner of its recorded inbound hold. Added end-to-end pressure, repeated composed HA refresh, missing-owner allocator isolation, and queue-full/sweep regressions plus controls.
