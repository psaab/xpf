# #16087 — DHCP relay aggregate token bucket starves legitimate clients (A10-17 fairness leg)

- **Timestamp**: 2026-10-23
- **Action**: The #5670 relay limiter was one aggregate token bucket per
  interface, so a sender sustaining >= `maximum-packet-rate` consumed
  every refill and other clients' DISCOVERs starved for the flood's
  duration. Added bounded per-sender fairness (`fairLimiter` in
  `pkg/dhcprelay/relay.go`): each distinct sender gets a sub-bucket at
  sustained `ceil(rate/256)` pps floored at 1 pps, burst >= 8. V4 key =
  chaddr peeked from the fixed RFC 951 wire header, v6 key = Client-ID
  DUID (no usable Client ID -> shared unknown-sender bucket). Table
  bounded at 256 senders/interface, longest-idle (>= 30 s) recycling;
  rotation cannot evict established senders. Fairness drops reuse
  `RequestsDroppedRateLimit` + the throttled warn-once log. The eviction
  leg of A10-17 stays refuted (pending capacity is rate-derived) and is
  untouched. Honest limit documented in the dhcprelay README: the key is
  client-chosen, so this stops a naive single-identity flooder (incl.
  the common looping-client accident), not per-packet identity
  rotation — that needs switch DHCP-snooping/per-port controls.
  **Fold (#16322, Opus)**: the first head refunded a fairness-denied
  packet's aggregate token. Removed — no refund exists. Fairness now
  gates BEFORE the aggregate (v4 key wire-peeked pre-parse; v6 fairness
  ahead of the aggregate for every non-Relay-Reply request, Relay-Reply
  dispatcher traffic keeps its pre-fold aggregate-only accounting), so
  a denied sender costs no aggregate token and the aggregate stays a
  hard pre-parse bound on parsed work; an aggregate denial after a
  fairness admission simply also spent the fairness token (strictly
  tighter). Second fold arm: per-sender share is floored (sustained
  >= 1 pps; burst lifted to the aggregate burst at rates <= 4 pps), so
  at `maximum-packet-rate 1` a sender completes a full DORA inside the
  burst instead of waiting ~256 s per packet. The burst lift is capped
  at the fairness floor for ordinary rates — an uncapped lift handed a
  lone flooder the whole aggregate burst (caught by the unit tests).
  **Fold 2 (#16322, Opus)**: (a) DHCPv6 previously parsed
  (`dhcpv6.FromBytes`) before both limiters. The v6 fairness key is now
  peeked from the wire options (`fairSenderKeyV6FromWire`, first
  Client-ID TLV, raw-DUID key verified byte-identical to the parsed
  `copyPending6DUID` encoding) and fairness+aggregate gate before
  `FromBytes`; Relay-Forw/Relay-Reply are routed by their type byte
  ahead of it (nested drop / dispatcher aggregate-only accounting
  unchanged). The DUID walk stops at a malformed TLV and lumps the
  packet into the unknown sender. (b) Rate-1 competition: the composed
  gate (`admitWithAggregate`) now banks the aggregate's accrual on
  every packet (`bankRefill` drains the bucket into a credit pool) and
  serves queued senders round-robin by ticket: one outstanding grant,
  queued senders spend no fairness token (`fairAdmit`), so at 1 pps a
  second sender is granted the second banked refill (~2 aggregate
  intervals) instead of racing the flooder for each refill token.
  `TestFairLimiter_Rate1TwoSenderRotation` pins the schedule
  deterministically (A burst 2, A granted refill #1, B granted refill
  #2 at step ~20 of 100 ms steps); `TestFairSenderKeyV6FromWire` pins
  the peek. A plain per-sender aggregate sub-bucket at 1/256 pps was
  rejected: it reintroduces the ~256 s strand the rate floor removed.
  **Fold 3 (#16322, Opus fold-2 ledger)**: (1) type-12 Relay-Forw was
  the last traffic reaching `FromBytes` unmetered — it now passes
  `admitWithAggregate(fairUnknownSenderV6, ...)` like every other
  non-Relay-Reply type; only Relay-Reply stays exempt. Pinned by
  `TestDHCPV6RelayForwardGatedByLimiter` (rate 1: burst 2 reach the
  nested drop, 4 count as rate-limit). (2) A granted ticket now
  expires after `fairTicketTTL` (2 s) on the next bank step, so an
  absent grantee cannot block the FIFO
  (`TestFairLimiter_TicketExpiryUnblocksFIFO`). (3) `joinRotation`
  requires a sender-table entry, so `len(waiters) <= len(table) <=
  256` unconditionally. (4) The banked credit pool is clamped to
  `relayBurstFor(rate)` after every bank
  (`TestFairLimiter_WaitersBoundedByTableAndCreditClamped`).
  **Fold 4 (#16322, Opus)**: `evictIdle` deleted the table entry but
  left the evicted key in `waiters`/`waiting` (orphan waiter breaking
  `len(waiters) <= len(table)`) and could leave it holding the ticket
  (phantom admission). Eviction now calls `leaveRotation` for the
  evicted key and forfeits its ticket (`TestFairLimiter_EvictionReconcilesRotation`:
  full table + 3 queued + ticketed head, idle past TTL, newcomer
  evicts the victim; no orphan waiter/ticket, invariant restored).
  **Fold 5 (#16322, Opus)**: (1) the fold-3 credit clamp counted only
  the pool; a paid-out ticket could push pool+ticket to burst+1. The
  clamp now reserves room for the outstanding ticket
  (`TestFairLimiter_PoolPlusTicketCapped`). (2) Queued/ticket shortcut
  paths admitted without refreshing `last`, so an actively-contending
  waiter went eviction-stale after `fairIdleEvict` and became the
  preferred victim; every admit attempt now touches its sender entry
  (`touch`, also inside `fairAdmit`)
  (`TestFairLimiter_QueuedSenderStaysEvictionFresh`).
  **Fold 6 (#16322, Opus, test-only)**: added
  `TestRunRelay_AggregateGate_MultiIdentity` — the aggregate gate's own
  binder, isolated from fairness. Four senders x 4 packets (each
  fairness-clean at 4 <= burst 8, sum 16 > aggregate burst 10),
  round-robin interleaved, frozen clock: exactly 10 relayed / 6
  dropped. Mutation verified both directions: removing ONLY the
  aggregate check inside `admitWithAggregate` (fairness intact) relays
  all 16 / drops 0 -> RED; removing fairness instead (control) stays
  GREEN, proving the aggregate is the isolated binding constraint.

  `TestRelayBindsTheKernelDevice9406`, `TestInterfaceIPv4_Loopback` —

- **Risk**: a lone sender is now capped at ~rate/256 sustained (1 pps at
  the 100 pps default, burst 8) instead of the full aggregate. Legit
  DHCP clients send a handful of packets per lease with 4/8/16 s
  retransmit backoff, well inside that; a client exceeding it is
  indistinguishable from the flooder this bounds. Operators on segments
  with legitimately chatty single clients can raise
  `maximum-packet-rate` (which raises every sender's share). No config
  schema, counter, or wire change.
