# Issue 11403 — preserve surviving ECMP flow pins on member loss

## Why

The selector chose `flow_hash % live_member_count` in candidate order. When a member disappeared, changing the divisor renumbered the live set and moved many re-resolved flows between still-healthy egresses. That can change ISP, zone, or redundancy-group choice even though the selected member itself remains available.

## What changed

- Replaced live-count modulo selection with allocation-free weighted rendezvous scoring (`-ln(U) / weight`) over stable, family-specific next-hop identity (gateway presence/address, ifindex, and tunnel endpoint id). Equal weights retain ordinary rendezvous ordering; score ties use stable hash and identity tie-breakers rather than candidate-vector position.
- Preserved the single liveness pass and no-live fallback precedence: prefer a drivable candidate, otherwise return a candidate from the full set. The same weighted ranking keeps fallback picks stable as candidates disappear.
- Added IPv4+IPv6 route-resolution cases covering 1,024 flows across three members with both default weights and [1, 4, 2], then removing the weight-4 member's neighbor. Only flows on the removed member may move. A selector cell separately checks weight-proportional placement, zero-weight normalization, and `u32::MAX` weights.

## Reproduction and validation

On clean base `094dcc1c4`, `ecmp_member_removal_preserves_survivors_v4_v6_11403` failed for IPv4 source port 1025: it moved from surviving egress 33 to egress 11 after the other member was removed.

## Risk

Only new or re-resolved route selections use rendezvous ranking; cached flow hits are unchanged. The stable member identity is local to ECMP selection and adds no allocation or wire/HA state. IPv4 and IPv6 share the same selection algorithm and retain their existing liveness and fallback behavior.
