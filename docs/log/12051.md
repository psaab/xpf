# #12051 — suppress unreachable TCP resets on stamped fabric ingress

## Why

Policy TCP rejects and Junos zone `tcp-rst` session-miss replies reflect the incoming Ethernet source MAC. A synthetic V1/V2 fabric stamp is not the client's L2 address, so an RST to it cannot reach the client. The V1-only policy gate also missed the preferred V2 stamp and did not cover the separate session-miss path; explicit rejects could report a sent REJECT despite the unreachable reply.

## What

A shared byte-prefix predicate recognizes V1
`02:bf:72:<FABRIC_ZONE_MAC_MAGIC>` or V2 `FABRIC_NAT_SCOPE_MAC_PREFIX`.
Neighbor learning skips both prefixes on any ingress because forwarded
fabric frames can enter its shared learning path elsewhere; TCP reply
suppression additionally requires configured fabric ingress.
`enqueue_reject_reply` suppresses stamped-fabric TCP before reply
construction or accounting, covering policy/filter rejects and zone
`tcp-rst` session misses. Policy events remain truthful DENY. Native
stamp-shaped TCP, unstamped-fabric TCP, and stamped-fabric UDP retain
their prior RST/port-unreachable behavior.

## Risk / residual

The suppression is limited to TCP on a configured fabric ingress when the source carries a V1/V2 synthetic stamp; native, unstamped-fabric, and UDP controls retain their existing behavior. No session-miss RST metric was added: the early unreplyable-frame exit preserves #3656 H11/H12 ordering and consumes no generated-reply token, budget, or output-filter accounting. No live-cluster validation was run; the poll-path behavior is covered by scoped tests.
