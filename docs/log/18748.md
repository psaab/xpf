### Cycle-3 design: the exact share is reserved at the root lease

Root cause. `SharedCoSRootLease` grants whoever asks first, up to the
outstanding cap `C = max_total_leased`. A worker may also keep credit in
`root.tokens` between drains. Cycles 1 and 2 guessed whether held credit
would be spent: cycle 1 with a predicate on local backlog, cycle 2 with a
200 us idle timer. Each guess has a counterexample:
- the crossed lease (cycle 1);
- a low-rate class whose credit is taken just before it wakes;
- a holder that trickles best-effort and so never reads as idle (cycle 2).
Cycle 3 stops guessing. Holding credit you cannot spend is impossible by
construction, and non-exact can never be granted the exact share of `C`.

Rule 1, a reservation at the lease:
- `SharedCoSRootLease::acquire_unreserved(now, requested, reserve)` is the
  same single CAS on the same packed word. Its headroom is `C - reserve`
  instead of `C`. There are no new atomics and no new shared fields.
- Exact service acquires with the existing `acquire` (full cap).
- Non-exact service (guarantee and surplus) acquires with
  `acquire_unreserved(R)`.
- `R = 0` when no exact demand is published. Non-exact then sees the full
  cap, so an idle exact class costs nothing.
- Under demand, `R = clamp(ceil(C * D / S), F, C)`, where:
  - `D` is the demanding exact rate (the mask the residual already uses);
  - `S` is the shaping rate;
  - `F = min(tx_frame_capacity, C / 2)`, so one frame always fits on
    either side.
- Invariant: a non-exact grant never raises total outstanding above
  `C - R`. While exact holders hold `X`, an exact request has at least
  `R - X` headroom.

Rule 2, no root credit held between drains. This applies on a root that
has an exact class.
- A worker acquires root only for the batch it is about to build, and only
  after that batch's own gate passes:
  - exact: queue-lease tokens >= head;
  - non-exact surplus: residual budget >= head;
  - guarantee: its tokens >= head.
- The request is sized to that batch (own tokens or budget, capped by the
  visit cap).
- Whatever the settle did not consume goes back
  (`release_unused`) before the drain returns. So `root.tokens == 0` on
  every worker between drains, idle workers included.
- Roots without an exact class keep today's prime-and-hold
  (`maybe_top_up_cos_root_lease`) byte for byte.

Removed:
- `reclaim_idle_root_credit`, `COS_ROOT_RECLAIM_NS` and
  `cos_root_may_top_up`;
- `root_reclaim_cooldown_until_ns`, `root_idle_since_ns` and
  `root_spent_bytes`/`root_spent_at_last_prime`, with the three settle
  increments;
- `hold_root_only_for_spendable_surplus` and `surplus_root_released`. A
  worker with no residual budget never acquires, so there is nothing to
  give back;
- the prime's top-up on exact-class roots. The prime still advances the
  timer wheel.

Kept:
- the demand mask;
- the shared residual pool with its #7206 guard;
- republish on change.

Strengthened: the parked-demand bit counts only while own tokens are at
least the head length recorded at park. A sub-head positive balance left
by a lease release withdraws the demand, not only a zero balance.

How each finding closes:
- Crossed lease, any poll ratio (cycle-1 F1, cycle-2 P1 #2). The cycle has
  two legs:
  - B holds root while it has no queue credit. Rule 2 makes this
    impossible: root is taken only after queue credit is in hand, and it
    is returned by the end of that drain.
  - E holds queue credit and waits on root. Rule 1 means E finds at least
    `R` of headroom.
  With one leg gone there is no cycle, whatever the cadence.
- Active best-effort holder (P1 #3). B's grants stop at `C - R`, and the
  residual budget bounds its draw rate. Available tokens therefore accrue
  at `D` or more, and E has `R` of headroom. There is no timer for B's
  sends to reset.
- Low rate and slow polling (P1 #1). Nothing is taken away on a clock. A
  single worker's exact class acquires when its own queue credit arrives,
  which is master behaviour (124,500 B/s at 1 Mb/s, 48 Mb/s for 50 of
  100).

Cost. On exact-class roots:
- at most one acquire CAS per batch (today it is at most one per drain
  below the lease target);
- one release CAS when a drain ends with unspent credit (TX ring short,
  budget cut).
Since this is a dataplane change, the loss throughput gate applies.

Cells and the mutant each must kill:
1. A lease unit property: random interleavings of exact and non-exact
   acquire, consume and release. Non-exact never pushes outstanding past
   `C - R`. Exact headroom is at least `R - X`. Mutant: non-exact uses the
   full cap.
2. No held credit: after every drain exit on an exact-class root (sent,
   TX ring full, budget 0, parked, empty), `root.tokens == 0`, and the
   lease's outstanding is 0 once every worker is idle. Mutant: skip the
   end-of-drain return.
3. Codex's probes (REVIEW-PROBES-OPTIMIZED.patch) as cells:
   - the crossed poll-period and phase matrix must drain all 9,000 bytes;

   window. It varies:
   - per-worker poll period (equal, 50/400 us, 500 us); the sim gains a
     per-worker period;
   - rate from 1 Mb/s to 24 Gb/s;
   - root burst (16k, 96k, default) and lease burst.

5. Re-park with a sub-head positive balance (head - 1 after a lease

   expected mask from the lengths it enqueued and the tokens it set, and

   backlog alone.
7. An idle exact class: with exact configured but idle, non-exact
   throughput equals master. Mutant: `R` sized from the configured exact
   rate instead of demand.
8. The frame floor: an exact class at 2% of a 16k-burst root, beside a
   non-exact holder, still sends 1,500-byte frames. Mutant: no `F`.

Open questions for the brief:
- Rule 2 scope. Static (the root has an exact class) or dynamic (exact
  demand is published)? With dynamic scope, an exact-idle interval stays
  identical to master, but credit held before the demand edge must be
  returned on that edge, and an idle worker never sees the edge. I
  recommend static.
- `F` on small bursts. At a 16,000-byte burst (`C = 4,000`), `F = 2,000`
  gives each side one 1,500-byte frame. A frame larger than `C / 2` cannot
  be guaranteed on such a root, which master already cannot do (#19051).
- Non-exact transmit-rate guarantees are on the unreserved side. If the
  exact rates plus those guarantees exceed shaping, the guarantees are
  squeezed to `C - R`.
- The v8 queue lease is unchanged. Rule 2 removes the root leg of the
  cycle, so the queue lease needs no change. #19031 stays separate.
- #19025's claims and colocated turn sit on the residual pool, not on
  root. They rebase onto cycle 3, and only the turn's root acquisition
  changes, to follow Rule 2.
