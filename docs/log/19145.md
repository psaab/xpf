# #19145: the RG-activation prewarm resurrects a session deleted during its fan-out

Lane F2. Branch `fix/19145-prewarm-fences`, on top of #18641 step 1
(`fix/18641-activation`). The two gate together as two commits in one
three-machine run (coordinator, 2026-11-16).

## The defect (from the issue)

The prewarm snapshots its candidates under the shared-session lock, releases
it, and then writes three things:
- the steering rows;
- the synthesized reverses, into the shared map;
- a snapshot `UpsertSynced` per worker.

A `sync_session` delete is served off the server lock (#452), so it can run
in between. Its removals and `DeleteSynced` pushes then sit behind the
prewarm's writes. At c61aa074f0 the repro (afb3120db8) fails 5 of 6
properties: an orphaned shared reverse, both steering rows, and both worker
rows.

## The design (coordinator-approved 2026-11-16, with three conditions)

**Rejected: the per-tuple lease.** `global_tuple_gate` is the CONNTRACK
map's admission gate. A publish claim needs the entry to be Idle and fails
fast otherwise. Holding it for steering writes would turn a concurrent HA
import's conntrack publish into `GateBusy`, and would skip rows whenever a
worker held an install permit.

**Rejected: publish-then-verify, or any check of shared presence alone.**
Both need every removal path to remove the shared row before deleting the
steering row. Four paths delete their WORKER-held steering rows first:
`session_delta.rs:890` and `:943`, `revalidation.rs:667` and `promote.rs:452`.

**Chosen: the steering registry's own contract (#9560 round 3).**
- **The contract:** an activation steering write is a COORDINATOR claim. A
  row is deleted only when its last owner goes, and every authoritative
  removal of a shared entry releases the coordinator claim. It removes the
  entry first, under the shared lock, and only then releases.
- **The census** (`release_coordinator_session_rows` after
  `remove_shared_session`):
  - `session_delta` 890→905 and 943→951;
  - `revalidation` 667→678;
  - `session_admission` 65→104;
  - `install_table_purge` 341→401 and 437→496;
  - `delete_synced` 2485→2664, with its coordinator steering delete.
- **What the resurrected row was:** a claim re-made after the release, with
  nothing left to release it again.

### The three layers

1. **Steering:** `publish_steering_while_shared` checks that the shared map
   still holds the snapshot's incarnation, and publishes (and claims) under
   ONE shared-lock hold.
   - A removal that ran first is seen, and nothing is written.
   - A removal that runs after releases the claim, because its release
     follows its removal.
   - It is used for the prewarm's forward and reverse writes, and for the
     republish, whose own collect-then-write window is the same race. Since
     #18641 step 1 the republish is the only activation writer of an
     owner-index forward's row.
2. **Shared reverse:** `publish_shared_session_if` admits the reverse only
   while the forward incarnation it was synthesized from is present,
   checked under the insert's own lock hold.
3. **Worker:** the prewarm pushes `WorkerCommand::PrewarmSynced { entry,
   shared }`. The worker applies it like `UpsertSynced`, but only while
   `shared` still holds that incarnation, checked when it applies it.
   - A delete before the check gets a skip.
   - A delete after the check pushed its `DeleteSynced` behind this command
     in the same FIFO, so it removes the install.
   - The shared map travels in the command as a `SharedSessionsHandle`,
     whose `Debug` prints nothing. That avoids threading a new parameter
     through `apply_worker_commands` and every one of its test callers.
   - B1 (#18641 step 2) later replaces the per-entry command with a key
     batch carrying the same handle.

"The same incarnation" is the rule `publish_shared_session` already uses:
presence is required, and a zero `session_id` on either side matches.

### Condition 1: lock order (shared-session → holdings shard → row shard)

The fence nests the steering registry's shard locks inside the shared-session
lock, which is a new nesting.
- **The census,** also written at `steering_owners.rs` `lock()`: every
  registry shard is taken through `lock()`. The only code run under one is
  the registry's bookkeeping and the caller closures in `bpf_map/mod.rs`:
  - `publish_session_rows`, `release_session_rows`,
    `publish_session_map_key`, `delete_live_session_key` and
    `retire_worker_steering_rows`;
  - and through them only `write_steering_row`, `delete_steering_row`,
    `session_key_for_row` and a slice position lookup.
  - None takes a shared-ops lock.
- **Enforced in debug builds:**
  - each registry shard guard (`ShardGuard`) counts itself on its thread;
  - `lock_shared_recover` and `lock_shared_publish` `debug_assert!` that
    the count is zero;
  - so every test run trips on a violation.
- **Cells:**
  - `a_shared_ops_lock_inside_a_registry_shard_trips_the_order_assertion_19145`
    (`should_panic`);
  - `the_registry_shard_count_returns_to_zero_19145`.

### Condition 2: cost

`activation_cost_bench_19145` is an ignored, release-mode bench:
- 8k sessions and 6 workers, with the recorder-only steering map, so there
  are no syscalls;
- 9 activate/demote rounds of RG1 through `update_ha_state`;
- it prints the wall time and the helper's phase line.

It runs on this tree and on #18641 step 1 alone. The difference is the
fences' CPU and lock cost. If the fences ate step 1's saving, the fallback
would be to take the shared lock per CHUNK and re-check each entry under one
hold.

**Result** (2026-11-16, 08:13:28Z to 08:13:32Z, udp:xpf-testrunner3,
release):
- **The setup:** both binaries were built on that runner. They ran
  alternately in the order ABBA ABBA AB, with A = step 1 alone (no fences)
  and B = this tree. That is 5 runs × 9 activations per arm, all 45 of
  each read.
- **The fixture:** 8000 sessions; every forward is in S, so the prewarm
  publishes 0 forward rows; 8000 reverses; 6 workers; a republish of 8000.
- **loadavg** at the end: 2.98 / 1.81 / 1.08.

| phase | A median µs | B median µs | B−A | A p25–p75 | B p25–p75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prewarm (total) | 21244 | 21860 | +616 | 19936–22173 | 20626–23612 |
| collect | 4538 | 4315 | −223 | 4270–4694 | 3919–4643 |
| forward steering (0 publishes) | 353 | 331 | −22 | 322–374 | 319–366 |
| forward fan-out | 2210 | 2810 | +600 | 2172–2266 | 2766–2891 |
| reverse shared | 6316 | 7078 | +762 | 6127–6578 | 6623–7611 |
| reverse steering | 4965 | 5055 | +90 | 4461–5305 | 4743–5522 |
| reverse fan-out | 1377 | 1610 | +233 | 1372–1388 | 1604–1627 |
| republish | 7172 | 7652 | +480 | 6590–7357 | 7071–8409 |

The whole `update_ha_state` call, as per-run medians: A 25245–29545
(median 28892), B 26938–32860 (median 29543).

**Reading:** the fences cost about **+1.1 ms per 8k-session activation** in
CPU and lock work:
- an `Arc` clone per `PrewarmSynced` push, about +0.8 ms over both
  fan-outs;
- the admit lookup, about +0.8 ms in reverse shared;
- the per-entry fence, about +0.5 ms in the republish;
- partly offset by noise, such as collect at −0.2.

Step 1's saving is the duplicate forward publishes, the same syscalls as a
republish, which #18654 measured at about 13-18 ms per RG. So **the fences
do not eat it**, and the per-chunk fallback is not needed.

**What the bench cannot see:** in production the fence holds the shared lock
across each steering syscall (about 1-2 µs each). Worker fallback reads at
takeover can wait for that. The A/B on tcp reports it through the
instrument's phase line and the resume guard.

**The cells, final:**
- two controls (`OwnerActivates`, `SplitReverseActivates`);
- a delete after the prewarm's snapshot, in both shapes;
- a delete after the prewarm's admitted reverse publish (seam
  `PREWARM_AFTER_REVERSE_PUBLISH_HOOK_19145`);
- a delete after the republish's snapshot;

**The first mutant pass** (five mutants) left M-steer-prewarm SURVIVING.

  delete at the snapshot seam never reaches that write.

  covers the prewarm's own forward write, which the republish does not
