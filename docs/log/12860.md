# Issue #12860 — keep scheduled ICMP denies in the session-hit type gate

## Why

Policy parsing built `icmp_type_constrained_term` only from rules whose snapshot
had `inactive == false`. That bit is stale once the scheduler heartbeat lease
expires: packet evaluation intentionally keeps scheduled Deny/Reject rules
eligible after expiry, even when their cached bit says inactive. Consequently,
the established-session owner/foreign-hit paths could miss an ICMP-specific deny
because their packet-type gate read an unarmed bitmap and trusted the cached
session verdict.

This is specifically a lease-expiry / existing-session transition. Initial
new-flow evaluation already considers eligible denies with the packet's actual
ICMP type; ordinary inactive rules remain skipped while the scheduler lease is
fresh.

## What

The packet-scoped constrained-term bitmap now includes inactive scheduled
Deny/Reject rules, which may become eligible when their lease expires. The
permit-only bitmap remains limited to active permits: expired scheduled permits
are skipped, and inactive permits do not create a type-blind false-deny risk.
Unscheduled inactive denies remain excluded because they cannot become eligible.

Regression coverage checks fresh-vs-expired lease behavior for Deny and Reject
with ICMPv4 and ICMPv6 type-specific terms, verifies the packet gate is armed
without arming the permit-only gate, and includes inactive-unscheduled and
active-scheduled controls.

## Evidence

- `userspace-dp/src/policy.rs:2720-2752` built both constrained-term
  bitmaps behind `!rule.inactive`; it now separately retains the permit-only
  condition and includes inactive scheduled Deny/Reject rules in the
  packet-scoped term bitmap.
- `userspace-dp/src/policy.rs:1861-1865` recognizes scheduled Deny/Reject
  rules as lease-sensitive; `:1908-1917` distinguishes the packet-scoped,
  action-inclusive predicate from the permit-only revalidation predicate.
- `userspace-dp/src/policy.rs:4401-4409` skips an inactive scheduled rule
  only while the lease is fresh; after lease expiry, scheduled permits are
  skipped but scheduled Deny/Reject rules remain eligible.
- `userspace-dp/src/afxdp/poll_descriptor/session_hit_authority.rs:263-274`
  and `:381-395` show the owner and foreign session-hit gate consumers. The
  owner evaluates the packet's actual ICMP type when the gate is armed; the
  foreign path avoids treating a constrained-deny miss as a flow-level permit.
- `userspace-dp/src/policy_tests.rs:629-735` pins fresh/expired results for
  inactive scheduled Deny and Reject rules across ICMPv4 and ICMPv6, alongside
  unscheduled-inactive and active-scheduled gate controls.

The claim is therefore **confirmed**, scoped to established-session hits and
scheduled inactive ICMP-specific Deny/Reject rules that become eligible when
their scheduler lease expires. New-flow policy matching is not the affected
path. The conservative pre-expiry packet gate can add work on a rare
inactive-scheduled-deny configuration; actual evaluation still skips that rule
while its lease is fresh.

## Risk / residual

Only a conservative snapshot-level packet-type gate changes. When an inactive
scheduled deny has an ICMP type constraint, established ICMP session hits may
now perform packet-scoped policy evaluation even before lease expiry, when the
rule remains inactive; this is required to ensure they are re-evaluated if the
scheduler lease expires without rebuilding the policy snapshot. The gate remains
protocol-family-specific and does not change fresh-lease rule eligibility or
the frame-independent permit-only revalidation doctrine.
