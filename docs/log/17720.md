# #17720 — require fragment-range coverage before releasing associations

- **Timestamp**: 2026-10-29
- **Action**: Track admitted fragment byte ranges and release an MF=0 association only after coverage reaches its terminal end.
- **File(s)**: `userspace-dp/src/afxdp/poll_descriptor/frag_assoc.rs`, `userspace-dp/src/fragment_assoc/key.rs`, `userspace-dp/src/fragment_assoc/mod.rs`, `userspace-dp/src/nat64_tests.rs`, `userspace-dp/src/afxdp/tests_fragment.rs`, `userspace-dp/src/afxdp/tests_nat64_tunnel.rs`, `debian/changelog`, `docs/log/17720.md`

## Why

MF=0 identifies the fragment with the datagram's greatest offset; it does not mean that fragment arrived after every lower-offset fragment. Releasing its association immediately lets a reordered middle fragment miss the admitted NAT/NAT64 decision and hit the fail-closed drop path.

## What

- Parse fragment byte ranges from the IPv4 total length or IPv6 payload length, and seed each association with its admitted head range.
- Maintain a bounded, allocation-free 8-byte-block coverage bitmap and terminal end per association. Only a covered prefix `[0, terminal_end)` releases the entry; incomplete, conflicting, truncated, or malformed geometry cannot trigger release.
- Both NAT64 and same-family/plain consult paths record ranges only after a successful, accepted association hit. A later middle fragment can finish coverage after the terminal fragment arrived first.
- Add cache-level and packet-level regressions for head-tail-middle retention and head-middle-tail completion, including both actual NAT64 and same-family/plain consult paths. Verify IPv6 range geometry selects the real non-atomic Fragment header after an atomic prefix.

## Risk

The fixed bitmap adds 1 KiB per cache entry, capped at 1 MiB across all 1,024 entries. Unknown fragment geometry conservatively leaves an association until ordinary TTL/quota reclaim rather than risking early release; packet translation and fail-closed miss behavior are unchanged.
