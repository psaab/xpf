## What the issue asked, and what the measurement changed

The issue reports that every un-projectable fine `to-zone junos-host` shape
commits clean with a warning and is unenforced on the direct kernel path, and it
rates the source-restricted / destination-restricted / application-restricted
PERMIT rows as gaps where "the coarse gate admits and the fine policy denies".

Measured first, because the fix follows from it:

- **The runtime has no implicit junos-host default-deny.**
  `evaluate_junos_host_policy_l3_aware` (userspace-dp/src/policy.rs) returns
  `None` on no match and the caller delivers; `policymatch.matchJunosHost` mirrors
  it and returns `HostInboundUnmatched`. Both name the management lifeline as the
  reason.
- So a restricted **permit with no deny after it** refuses nothing on the
  userspace path either. Those rows are a Junos-parity difference, deliberately
  taken, not a kernel-vs-runtime gap — and the warning claiming the restriction
  "is enforced only on the userspace AF_XDP local-delivery path" was false for
  them.
- The rows where the kernel really did diverge from xpf's own runtime are:
  `then reject`, a deny on a `tcp-rst` zone, and **any deny sitting behind a
  permit the subtraction model could not express** — which is the canonical
  management ACL (`permit <mgmt-net> junos-ssh; deny any`).

## The change

- **First-match subchain.** Every zone program renders into its own nft chain,
  entered by an `iifname`-scoped `jump`. `deny` → drop, `deny` on a `tcp-rst`
  zone → TCP RST then drop, `reject` → TCP RST / ICMP admin-prohibited, `permit`
  → `return`. End of chain returns, matching the runtime's deliver-on-no-match.
  A `return` is not an accept: the coarse gate still decides.
- The verdicts mirror `enqueue_deny_reply` / `enqueue_policy_reject_reply`
  (userspace-dp/src/afxdp/poll_descriptor/reject_reply.rs) rather than coarsening
  everything to a silent drop.
- Set subtraction, the poison sentinel and `PermitSubtract` are gone.
- Warning suppression now covers `reject` as well as `deny`; a permit is still
  never suppressed, and its message was rewritten to say what is actually true.

## Residuals

- The nft `reject` statement is not rate-limited the way the helper's synthesized
  reply is (`allow_generated_reject`); the kernel's own ICMP rate limit applies to
  the ICMP arm, and the RST arm matches what a closed port answers.
- Scheduler-gated, feed-tainted, ALG/mixed-application and IPsec/ident-tuple
  shapes remain un-representable and keep the warning.
