## The two things that were wrong

The #8618 gate that declines ICMP re-derivation arms only on type-constrained
**PERMIT** rules (`policy.rs`, `icmp_type_constrained_permit`). But
`CompiledApplications::matches` fails a type-constrained term closed for **any**
action when `packet_icmp` is `None`, and the frame-independent re-derivation always
passes `None`. So a type-constrained ICMP **DENY** is skipped on the
established-session path: the walk falls through to a broader permit, the flow is
judged permitted, and the session is stamped revalidated.

1. **Two source comments asserted verdict EQUIVALENCE, which is stronger than what
   is true and is FALSE in the constrained-DENY case.** `policy.rs` said that with
   no type-constrained permit anywhere, `None` "yields exactly the verdict a
   fully-informed evaluation would, because `packet_icmp` is read in exactly one
   arm ... and that arm is inert when no term populates it".
   `policy_revalidation.rs` repeated it. A constrained DENY term DOES populate that
   arm; the predicate tests only for a constrained PERMIT. A reader relying on
   those comments concludes the type-blind evaluation is exact.
2. **The cell that appeared to bind this was vacuous for its second half.**
   `a_type_constrained_deny_does_not_suppress_the_icmp_re_derivation_8618` copied
   its deny from `junos_ping_permit()`, which is `dmz -> wan`, while the driven
   session is `lan -> wan` and the base fixture has no `lan -> wan` permit — so the
   flow revoked by DEFAULT-DENY whether or not the constrained deny was consulted.
   Only its first half (the direct predicate assertion) bound anything.

## The contract decision: ACCEPT, and why

#9386 offers Enforce or Accept. The decision taken here is **ACCEPT the residual,
correct the claim, and pin the behaviour**, and the reasoning is recorded because
it is the part a future reader will want to re-examine:

- **Widening the arming to any type-constrained TERM does not change the forwarding
  outcome.** Under it, gate 1b declines and the session survives unstamped; under
  the current arming the walk derives Permit and the session survives stamped.
  Either way the constrained deny is not enforced on the established path. What the
  widening DOES cost is real: the predicate is whole-snapshot, so one constrained
  deny anywhere would decline ICMP re-derivation box-wide and give up #8356's
  coverage for every ICMP flow on that box.
- **Supplying the packet's type/code is the only thing that closes it**, and it
  contradicts this derivation's own contract: it is deliberately
  frame-INDEPENDENT, because one packet's type is not the flow's property. The
  module header's original reasoning for that — *"stamping one packet's type as the
  flow's verdict would be just as wrong in the other direction"* — still holds.
- The sound, weaker guarantee IS sufficient for what the gate is for: a type-blind
  walk can never MANUFACTURE a false DENY, because skipping a constrained term can
  only fall through to a later, more permissive rule. So acting on a DENY is safe;
  a PERMIT is simply not a claim that a fully-informed walk would agree.

Dissent recorded: this leaves a real fail-OPEN — an operator's
`deny ... application junos-ping` ahead of a broad permit is not enforced on live
ICMP sessions. It is mitigated by the new-flow path (which DOES enforce it) and by
`security policies policy-rematch` at commit, and is now a stated, tested decision
rather than an unnoticed residual.
