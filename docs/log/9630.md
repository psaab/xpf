## 2. Design

The Go event stream now queues a FullResync marker and starts one detached
worker for the potentially long callback. The reader continues consuming
frames while `handleEventStreamFullResync` performs its paged
`ExportOwnerRGSessionsPaged` work. Additional barriers remain as FIFO markers,
but coalesce behind the active export. Their markers are retired only after the
preceding successful export, so cumulative ACKs cannot advance over unapplied
session frames. A callback returning false leaves the barrier pending.

Callback replacement, reconnect queue clearing, and `Close` each advance a
resync generation. Worker completion checks that generation before changing the
pending queue or ACK watermark. Close does not join a worker that may be
waiting on daemon session locks; a closed-worker bit prevents its completion
from flushing stale work onto a subsequently restarted listener. The callback
and generation publication is synchronized as one replacement boundary.

The synthetic session-gap, decode-failure, and telemetry-gap recovery paths
retain their established inline callback semantics because their watermark
advancement and rate-limiting contracts differ from the wire FullResync
barrier. The reader offload is scoped to the wire barrier that performs the
paged bulk export.

In the Rust helper, `SessionDelta` provenance now suppresses the complete
event-stream branch only for `ExportProvenance::CommandExport(_)`. A command
export already returned the authoritative paged control response, so emitting
its HA and RT_FLOW event-stream echo duplicates the response and can create a
replay-gap cascade. Binding RPC fallback, shared/recent-delta consumers, and
Incremental/LossResync event-stream deliveries remain active; LossResync still
emits its HA and RT_FLOW frames.
