# Issue #13934: Preserve L4 checksums on tunnel-egress fragments

- **Issue:** The copy builder's forced tunnel-egress L4 checksum recompute ran on offset-zero IPv4/IPv6 fragments because its guard only recognized non-first fragments. Recomputing from a first fragment, whose checksum covers the complete transport datagram, overwrote the sender's checksum with one computed over only the fragment; the reassembled TCP/UDP datagram then failed checksum validation.
- **Change:** Skip full inner L4 recomputation for any IPv4 fragment and non-atomic IPv6 fragments, using `ipv4_is_any_fragment` / `ipv6_is_nonatomically_fragmented`. IPv6 atomic fragments are complete datagrams and retain forced recompute behavior. Keep unfragmented tunnel behavior unchanged. Add first-fragment tests seeded from complete TCP datagrams, RFC-aligned fragment payloads, reassembly checksum verification, and an atomic IPv6 recompute control; retain non-first and unfragmented controls.
- **Validation:** On pristine guard logic, IPv4 and IPv6 first-fragment cells failed because the builder rewrote their checksums (2 failed, 2 controls passed). After the initial fix, the complete `tests_fragment_term_extra` module passed (35 tests). After refining IPv6 to preserve atomic-fragment recomputation and making first-fragment fixtures RFC-aligned with checksum verification after reassembly, the module passed again (35 tests).
- **Risk:** Fragmented packets bypass full checksum recomputation and preserve the sender's checksum for reassembly. IPv6 atomic fragments remain in the complete-datagram recompute path.
